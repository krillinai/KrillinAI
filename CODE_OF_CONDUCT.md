# OpenCreator Code of Conduct

OpenCreator welcomes people contributing code, Skills, templates, design, documentation, translations, bug reports, and ideas. We want these spaces to be useful and respectful for everyone, regardless of background or experience.

## Our standards

In project spaces, we expect participants to:

- Be respectful and considerate, especially when disagreeing or giving feedback.
- Discuss ideas and work on their merits, and assume good faith while seeking clarification.
- Accept constructive feedback and take responsibility for mistakes.
- Respect other people's time, privacy, and boundaries.
- Help keep discussions focused and welcoming to new contributors.

The following behavior is unacceptable:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, or sustained personal attacks.
- Discriminatory or derogatory language or conduct directed at a person or group.
- Unwanted sexual attention or sexualized content in project spaces.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberate disruption, repeated bad-faith engagement, or retaliation against someone who reports a concern.

## Scope

This code applies to OpenCreator's repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, and project-operated community spaces. It also applies when someone represents OpenCreator in a public project event or channel.

## Reporting and response

If you experience or witness a violation, contact an OpenCreator community moderator by direct message in the [Discord community](https://discord.gg/3GwBGsjs8). For content hosted on GitHub, you can also use GitHub's **Report content** action. Please do not include sensitive personal details in a public issue or pull request.

Share links, screenshots, and relevant context when possible. Maintainers will review reports as promptly as practical, limit access to the information to people handling the report, and avoid sharing the reporter's identity without consent except where necessary for safety or legal reasons. We cannot promise absolute confidentiality.

Depending on the circumstances, maintainers may ask someone to change their behavior, remove content, issue a warning, temporarily restrict participation, or permanently ban a participant from project spaces. They will consider the severity and pattern of behavior and may consult the people affected before deciding on a response. Retaliation for a good-faith report is itself a violation.
